# COURSE slav | Slav Defence | black
sub: 1...d5 and 2...c6 against 1.d4, one easy set-up against the London and other systems, and the same ideas against 1.Nf3 and 1.c4
why: The Slav is the Caro-Kann's brother. The pawn on c6 holds d5, and the light-squared bishop comes out to f5 or g4 before you play ...e6, so it is never locked in. That is exactly the plan you already know from your Caro-Kann. Against best play the Slav is one of the most solid answers to 1.d4: you get equal positions with a clear plan and very few ways to lose quickly. Your old King's Indian games were sharp and risky; here you will usually have a safe king and easy development.

## CHAPTER main | Main line: 4...dxc4 5.a4 Bf5
> White plays the most natural moves: c4, Nf3 and Nc3. You take the c4 pawn, and when White stops ...b5 with a4, you give the pawn back for quick development: ...Bf5, ...e6, ...Bb4 and castle. The rule of thumb: the bishop goes out to f5 before ...e6, exactly as in your Caro-Kann.
