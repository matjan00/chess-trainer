sub: 1.e4 c6 and 2...d5 against everything: a solid repertoire where your light-squared bishop gets out before ...e6
why: The Caro-Kann is already your most-played answer to 1.e4, and it suits you: the pawn on c6 supports ...d5, your pawn structure stays healthy, and the plans repeat from line to line. In your games White most often plays 2.d4 (25 games), then 2.Nf3 (18), 2.Bc4 (5), 2.Qf3 and 2.f4 (2 each); every one of them is in this course. ## CHAPTER adv-short | Advance Variation: quiet set-ups (3.e5 Bf5)
> White pushes the pawn to e5 and grabs space. You answer with the Caro-Kann rule: bishop out to f5 first, then ...e6, and then attack d4 with ...c5, ...Nc6 and ...Qb6. Your bishop is outside the pawn chain, so you get a good French Defence.
