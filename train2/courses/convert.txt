## CHAPTER convert | Converting an advantage | theme=defend
> Seven lessons, one rule each. The examples are moments from your own games where the win slipped away. The cards are your own positions too.

### LESSON stillgame | Winning is not won
goal: Keep asking about his threats when you are ahead.
rule: When you are ahead, your first question is still: what is his threat?
> When you are winning, it is natural to relax and look only at your own plans. That is exactly when his one trick works. Before each move, look at his pieces first: what can he check, capture or attack next? Then make your move.

### LESSON trade | Trade pieces, keep pawns
goal: Make the win simpler by swapping pieces when you are ahead.
rule: Ahead in material: swap pieces (not pawns), every fair trade makes the win simpler.
> When you are a piece up, every trade of equal pieces makes your extra piece count for more. Fewer pieces also means fewer tricks for him. So when he offers a fair trade, usually take it. Keep your pawns: they are what you will promote in the end.

### LESSON counterplay | Kill his counterplay first
goal: Stop his one active idea before you go after more.
rule: Before you win more, take away his only active idea: the open file, the passed pawn, the attack on your king.
> When you are winning, he usually has just one hope: a passed pawn, a rook on your back rank or checks against your king. Find that one idea and stop it. After that, your extra material wins by itself.

### LESSON takefree | Take what is free — after a check for the catch
goal: Grab free material, but only after checking that it is really free.
rule: Free material makes the win easier, if nothing is hanging at home.
> More material makes winning easier, so do not be shy: if something is free, take it. But "free" needs one check. Is the piece really undefended? Does your capture leave something of yours hanging? And is there a bigger thing to take?

### LESSON allpieces | Bring everyone
goal: Use all your pieces, and your king, before the final attack.
rule: Before the final attack, the worst-placed piece joins; in the endgame that includes the king.
> An attack with two pieces often runs out of steam; with three it usually wins. Before you start the final push, find your worst piece: the one doing nothing. Bring it closer first. In the endgame the king is a strong piece, so walk it towards the action.

### LESSON simplest | Choose the simplest win
goal: Prefer the safe, simple way to win.
rule: A clearly won endgame you know beats a flashy attack you have to calculate.
> When you are winning, you do not need the fastest win, only a safe one. Swapping into an endgame you know is often the best way. The Essential endgames course shows the ones to know: mate with queen or two rooks, the outside passed pawn, and the Lucena position.

### LESSON finish | Finish the job
goal: Turn a big advantage into checkmate, and never give stalemate.
rule: Far ahead: look for mate with checks, and never stalemate.
> When you are far ahead, look at every check first: often there is a mate. And before every move, look at his king once more. If it has no move and he has no other move, it is stalemate, a draw. The Essential endgames lesson on stalemate shows the trap.
