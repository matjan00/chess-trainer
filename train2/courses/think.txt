# COURSE think | Think first
sub: One short routine before every move, so that simple tactics stop costing you games.
why: In your 175 analysed games, 31% of your mistakes were played in under 5 seconds. Your opponent's best answer to your mistake was a check 50 times, and you left a piece hanging 49 times. Most of these were easy to see: you did not look. This course builds one routine and trains it until it is automatic.

## CHAPTER routine | The routine | theme=hanging
> Six short lessons. Each one adds one question to ask before you move. The cards come from your own games.

### LESSON check3 | The three questions
goal: Stop leaving pieces where he can take them for free.
rule: Before you let go of the piece, ask: after my move, what can he check, capture or threaten?
> Most of your lost pieces were not deep tactics. Your opponent just took something, often with check. The cure is three quick questions about his next move, asked before you let go of the piece. Checks first, then captures, then threats. It takes ten seconds and you have ten minutes.

### LESSON hismove | What does his move want?
goal: Read his last move before you choose yours.
rule: Every move changes something: what does it attack now, and what did it stop defending?
> Your opponent's move is a message. Before you think about your plans, read it. Which piece moved? What does it attack from its new square? Which line did it open behind it? One look at his last move tells you most of his threats.

### LESSON loose | Loose pieces drop off
goal: Spot your undefended pieces before he does.
rule: An undefended piece is a target for forks and double attacks: defend it or know why not.
> A loose piece is one that nothing defends. On its own it is fine. But a fork or a check with a capture hits two things at once, and the loose one falls. Before you move, count your loose pieces. If one of them can be hit together with something else, fix that first.

### LESSON checks | Never be surprised by a check
goal: See his checks before you move a piece away from your king.
rule: List his checks before you move a piece away from your king or open a line.
> In 50 of your mistakes, his best answer was a check. You must answer a check, so he gets a free move: to take a loose piece, bring another attacker or win time. Before you move, name every check he could give after your move. It takes five seconds.

### LESSON gift | Is it a gift or a trap?
goal: Punish his blunders, and do not fall for his bait.
rule: When he leaves something, take ten seconds: usually it is a blunder, so punish it; sometimes it is bait.
> Players at your level blunder often, so his mistakes are your best chance to win. When his move leaves something hanging, stop. Ask: can I take it, or hit two things at once? Then ask the three questions about your capture. If they come back clean, it was a gift: take it.

### LESSON clock | Where your time should go
goal: Spend your thinking time on the moves that decide the game.
rule: Spend time when the position changes: captures, checks, pawn breaks, and whenever you are winning.
> In your games the median time on a mistake was 7.4 seconds, and on your other moves 4.5 seconds. So you play your mistakes almost as fast as your normal moves. Quick moves are fine when nothing has changed. After a capture, a check or a pawn break, or when you are winning, stop and run the three questions.
