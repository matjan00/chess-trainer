# COURSE endgames | Essential endgames
sub: The endings every club player must know, checked move by move with the tablebase.
why: Many of your games are decided in the endgame, and most of them reach one of a handful of standard positions. Learn these and you stop throwing away won endings and stop losing drawn ones.

## CHAPTER mates | Basic checkmates | theme=mate2
> A queen or a rook up is only a win if you can deliver mate. These four lessons give you a fixed method for each mate, and show the stalemate traps that turn a win into a draw.

### LESSON qmate | Queen checkmate
goal: Mate with king and queen against king, quickly and safely.
rule: Shrink his box with the queen a knight's move away, bring your king close, then mate on the edge.
> The queen alone cannot mate: your king must help. Use the queen to shrink the box the enemy king lives in, often from a knight's move away, so that he is never stalemated. When he is stuck on the edge, walk your king up and give mate. In the corner, look for the mate before anything else.

#### EX qmate-a | 8/8/8/3k4/8/8/8/K5Q1 w - - 0 1 | src=book
> White to move. The black king is in the middle of the board.
1. Qe1 {The queen cuts off the e-file: the king must stay on the left side. [g:g1-e1]} Kc6 2. Qe6+ Kb7 3. Qd7+ {Checks drive him towards the edge.} Ka6 4. Kb1 {Now the king walks up to help.} Kb6 5. Kb2 Ka5 6. Qc6! {A knight's move away from his king: it shrinks the box and leaves him a square. [g:d7-c6]} Kb4 7. Qd5! {Again a knight's move away.} Ka4 8. Kc3 Ka3 9. Qb3# {Your king guards the queen and his king has nowhere to go.}
key: Queen a knight's move from his king, your king up close, then mate.

### LESSON qstale | Don't stalemate him
goal: Finish a won ending without giving stalemate.
rule: Before every quiet move, check that his king still has a legal move.
> Stalemate means the side to move is not in check but has no legal move, and the game is a draw. It happens most when you are a queen up and play fast. Before each move that is not a check, ask one question: after this, can his king still move? If not, look for a check instead.

#### EX qstale-a | 7k/8/5K2/8/8/8/8/6Q1 w - - 0 1 | src=book
> White to move. The natural ((1.Qg6)) is stalemate: the king on h8 cannot move and is not in check.
1. Qg5! {This leaves the king the h7-square. [g:g1-g5]} Kh7 2. Qg7# {The queen is guarded by your king: mate.}
key: Leave his king one square until you can give mate.

#### CARD qstale-1 | 8/8/8/8/8/6Q1/5K1p/7k w - - 0 1 | src=composed | goal=win
sol: Qg2#
also: Qf3# | Also mate, along the long diagonal.
why: Here a check is the only way: every quiet move leaves his king without a move, and that is stalemate.
wrong: Kf3 | ((1.Kf3)) A quiet king move, and Black has no legal move: stalemate.

#### CARD qstale-2 | 8/8/8/8/8/1Q6/p1K5/k7 w - - 0 1 | src=composed | goal=win
sol: Qb2#
also: Qc3# | The check on the long diagonal is mate as well.
why: His king is boxed in and his pawn cannot move. Only a check keeps the win: the queen mates on b2, guarded by your king.
wrong: Qa3 | ((1.Qa3)) It looks strong, but Black has no move at all: stalemate.

#### CARD qstale-3 | 8/8/8/8/8/8/7p/4QK1k w - - 0 1 | src=composed | goal=win
sol: Qe4#
also: Kf2# | The king steps aside and the queen gives mate along the first rank.
why: Give check with the queen on the long diagonal. A quiet queen move here takes his last squares away.
wrong: Qe2 | ((1.Qe2)) No check, no legal move for Black: stalemate.

### LESSON rrmate | Two-rook ladder
goal: Mate with two rooks without the help of your king.
rule: One rook fences a line, the other checks on the next line; keep both far from his king.
> Two rooks mate on their own, like climbing a ladder. One rook holds a rank, the other checks on the next rank, and they take turns. If his king comes close to a rook, move that rook far away along its line. Near the corner, watch out for stalemate.

#### EX rrmate-a | 8/8/3k4/8/8/8/8/R3K2R w - - 0 1 | src=book
> White to move. Your king can stay at home.
1. Ra5 {The first rook fences off the fifth rank. [g:a1-a5]} Ke6 2. Rh6+ {The second rook checks on the next rank.} Kd7 3. Ra7+ {The rooks take turns, one rank higher each time.} Kc8 4. Rh8# {The last rung of the ladder: mate on the back rank.}
key: Rooks take turns: one holds a rank, the other checks one rank further.

wrong: Rg4 | ((1.Rg4)) No check, and his king cannot move: stalemate.

#### CARD rrmate-2 | 8/8/8/1K1R2R1/8/8/7p/7k w - - 0 1 | src=composed | goal=win

### LESSON rmate | Rook checkmate
goal: Mate with king and rook against king.
rule: The rook cuts his king off; your king walks up to face his; then the rook checks him back a line.
> King and rook against king is a win, but only if your king helps. The rook builds a fence along a rank or file. Your king walks up until the two kings face each other with one square between. Then a rook check pushes him back one line. Keep the rook far from his king, or he just takes it.

#### EX rmate-a | 4k3/R7/8/8/8/8/8/4K3 w - - 0 1 | src=book
> White to move. The rook on the seventh rank keeps the black king on the back rank.
1. Kd2 {The rook does the fencing, so your king walks up.} Kd8 2. Kc3 Kc8 3. Rh7! {His king came close to the rook, so the rook moves far away along its rank. [g:a7-h7]} Kb8 4. Kc4 Kc8 5. Kd5 Kd8 6. Ke6 Kc8 7. Kd6 Kb8 8. Kc6 Ka8 9. Kb6! {Now the kings face each other on the b-file. [g:c6-b6]} Kb8 10. Rh8# {The rook checks along the back rank and the king has no square left.}
key: Fence with the rook, walk the king up to face his, then check along the edge.

#### CARD rmate-1 | 4k3/8/2K5/8/8/8/8/5R2 w - - 0 1 | src=book | goal=win
sol: Kd6
why: Step in front of his king so the kings face each other on the 6th and 8th ranks. If he goes to d8, the rook check on the back rank ends it.
wrong: Rf8+ | ((1.Rf8+ Kxf8)) The rook stood next to his king and is lost.

#### CARD rmate-2 | 3k4/8/5K2/8/8/8/8/2R5 w - - 0 1 | src=book | goal=win
sol: Ke6
why: The same pattern on the other side: your king faces his, and the rook waits on the c-file for the back-rank check.
wrong: Rc8+ | ((1.Rc8+ Kxc8)) A check next to his king just gives the rook away.

#### CARD rmate-3 | 8/8/8/8/3R4/1k6/8/2K5 w - - 0 1 | src=book | goal=win
sol: Kd2
why: The rook already fences him in. Bring your king closer; the rook alone cannot finish the job.
wrong: Rb4+ | ((1.Rb4+ Kxb4)) The rook checks from next to his king and is taken.

#### CARD rmate-4 | 8/8/8/8/8/k7/3R4/3K4 w - - 0 1 | src=book | goal=win
sol: Rd4
why: Shrink the box. With the rook on the fourth rank and your king guarding the first two ranks, his king is caught in the corner.
wrong: Ra2+ | ((1.Ra2+ Kxa2)) A check from next to his king loses the rook.

## CHAPTER kp | King and pawn | theme=pawnEndgame
> With only kings and pawns left, every single move counts. These lessons give you the counting tools: the square, key squares and the opposition. Everything later in the course is built on them.

### LESSON square | The square rule
goal: Know at a glance whether a king can catch a passed pawn.
rule: Picture a square with the pawn's path as one side; if the king can step inside it, he catches the pawn.
> A passed pawn far from both kings starts a race. Counting moves one by one is slow and easy to get wrong in a 10-minute game. The square is a shortcut: the pawn's path to the last rank is one side of it. If the defending king can step into that square on his move, the pawn is caught. A pawn on its starting rank counts from one square further up, because it can jump two squares.

#### EX square-a | 8/8/8/2P5/6k1/8/8/K7 b - - 0 1 | src=book
> Black to move. The square of the c5-pawn has the corners c5, f5, f8 and c8.
1... Kf5! {One diagonal step and the king is inside the square. [g:g4-f5] [r:c5] [r:f5] [r:f8] [r:c8]} 2. c6 Ke6 {The square shrinks as the pawn moves, and the king stays inside it.} 3. c7 Kd7 4. c8=Q+ Kxc8 {The pawn is caught and the game is a draw.}
key: Do not count moves: check whether the king can step into the square.

#### EX square-b | 8/8/6k1/8/1P6/8/8/7K w - - 0 1 | src=book
> White to move. Right now the black king could still step into the square of the b4-pawn.
1. b5! {Push at once. The new square runs from b5 to e8, and the king on g6 cannot reach it in one step. [g:b4-b5]} Kf6 2. b6 Ke6 3. b7 Kd7 4. b8=Q {The pawn queens: the king was one step too slow.}
key: When the king is just outside the square, push the pawn now before he steps in.

#### CARD square-2 | K7/8/8/8/7P/2k5/8/8 b - - 0 1 | src=book | tag=hold | goal=draw

#### CARD square-3 | 7K/8/8/8/8/8/P5k1/8 b - - 0 1 | src=book | tag=hold | goal=draw

### LESSON kpfront | King in front of the pawn
goal: Win king and pawn against king by leading with your king.
rule: Put your king two squares in front of the pawn, or next to that square, and the pawn will queen.
> The three squares two steps in front of a pawn are its key squares: with your king on one, you win. So the king goes first and the pawn follows. Pushing the pawn too early is the classic way to turn a win into a draw.

#### EX kpfront-a | 3k4/8/8/3K4/3P4/8/8/8 w - - 0 1 | src=book
> White to move. The key squares of the d4-pawn are c6, d6 and e6.
1. Kd6! {The king steps onto a key square. Now the pawn cannot be stopped. [g:d5-d6] [r:c6] [r:d6] [r:e6]} Ke8 2. Kc7 {The king clears the path. Black cannot get back in front of the pawn.} Ke7 3. d5 {Only now does the pawn move, protected all the way.} Kf7 4. d6 Ke6 5. d7 Ke7 6. d8=Q+ Ke6 {The pawn has queened.}
key: King first, pawn second: walk the king to a key square, then push.

#### CARD kpfront-1 | 4k3/8/8/3K4/4P3/8/8/8 w - - 0 1 | src=book | goal=win
sol: Ke6
also: Kd6 | Also a key square; both win.
why: The king steps onto a key square in front of the pawn, and Black can no longer stop it.
wrong: e5 | ((1.e5 Ke7)) The pawn went first, Black's king stands in front of it and it is a draw.

#### CARD kpfront-2 | 2k5/8/8/1K6/2P5/8/8/8 w - - 0 1 | src=book | goal=win
sol: Kc6
also: Kb6 | Also a key square of the pawn; both win.

wrong: c5 | ((1.c5 Kc7)) Black's king blocks the pawn and you cannot win.

#### CARD kpfront-3 | 6k1/8/8/5K2/6P1/8/8/8 w - - 0 1 | src=book | goal=win
sol: Kf6
also: Kg6 | The other key square in front of the pawn; both win.
why: Same rule on the other wing: put the king two squares in front of the pawn.
wrong: g5 | ((1.g5 Kg7)) The pawn moved first and Black's king gets in front of it.

#### CARD kpfront-4 | 3k4/8/3K4/3P4/8/8/8/8 w - - 0 1 | src=book | goal=win

### LESSON oppwin | The opposition: win
goal: Use the {gl:opposition} to force your king through.
rule: When the kings face each other with one square between, the side that must move has to give way.
> This standoff is called the opposition: the kings face each other with one square between them, and whoever must move gives way. If you take the opposition, his king has to step aside and yours walks forward. When he steps aside, go round the other side: that is called outflanking.

#### EX oppwin-a | 8/8/8/3k4/8/3K4/3P4/8 b - - 0 1 | src=book

1... Kc5 {Black steps aside.} 2. Ke4! {Go round the other side. Your king heads for the squares in front of the pawn. [g:d3-e4]} Kd6 3. Kd4! {Take the opposition again: now Black must give way once more. [g:e4-d4]} Kc6 4. Ke5 Kd7 5. d3 {A spare pawn move. The pawn stays back until the king has done its work.} Kc6 6. d4 Kd7 7. Kd5! {The opposition once more, now on the fifth rank.} Ke7 8. Kc6 Ke8 9. Kc7 {The king controls the queening square. The pawn walks through.} Ke7 10. d5 Kf6 11. d6
key: Take the opposition, and when his king steps aside, go round the other side.

### LESSON oppdraw | The opposition: hold
goal: Hold king and pawn against king as the defender.
rule: Stay in front of the pawn and face his king every time it steps forward.
> Now you are the defender. You hold the draw if you keep the opposition: the kings face each other with one square between, and he must move. When he steps up, step up to face him. When the pawn checks you, step straight back, never to the side.

Kc5 Kc7! 8. d6+ Kd7 9. Kd5 Kd8! {Straight back again, so you can face his king next move.} 10. Ke6 Ke8! 11. d7+ Kd8 12. Kd6 {Stalemate: Black has no legal move, so it is a draw.}
key: Face his king when it steps forward, and step straight back after a pawn check.

### LESSON kpdef | Get in front of the pawn
goal: Defend king and pawn against king by blocking the pawn's path.
rule: Run your king to the squares in front of the pawn; from there you can always face his king.
> The defending king belongs on the pawn's path, in front of it. From there you can face the white king whenever it steps forward, and the pawn never gets through. A king beside or behind the pawn is usually too late.

#### EX kpdef-a | 8/8/8/3k4/8/8/3P4/3K4 b - - 0 1 | src=book
> Black to move. Your king is already in front of the pawn: keep it there.
1... Kd4 {Stay on the pawn's path.} 2. Ke2 Ke4! {Face the king: one square between, and White must give way. [g:d4-e4]} 3. d3+ Kd4 4. Kd2 Kd5! {After the check, step back but stay in front of the pawn.} 5. Ke3 Ke5! {Face the king again.} 6. d4+ Kd5 7. Kd3 Kd6 8. Ke4 Ke6! {The same pattern one rank higher. White makes no progress.}
key: Keep your king in front of the pawn and face his king whenever it steps up.

## CHAPTER rooks | Rook endings | theme=rookEndgame
> Rook endings are the endings you will reach most often. A few rules decide most of them: rooks belong behind passed pawns, an active rook beats a passive one, and there are three positions every player must know by heart.

### LESSON lucena | Lucena: build a bridge
goal: Win the most important rook ending: pawn on the seventh, your king in front of it.
rule: Check his king away, put your rook on the fourth rank, walk your king out, and block the checks with the rook.
> This is the winning position every rook ending aims for. Your king sits in front of the pawn on the seventh, and his rook keeps checking. The plan: drive his king one more file away with a check and lift your rook to the fourth rank. Then walk your king out and block the checks with the rook. That block is called building a bridge.

#### EX lucena-a | 6K1/4k1P1/8/8/8/8/7r/5R2 w - - 0 1 | src=book
> White to move. This is the Lucena position.
1. Re1+! {First push his king one more file away. [g:f1-e1]} Kd7 2. Re4! {The bridge: the rook goes to the fourth rank. [g:e1-e4]} Rg2 3. Kf7 Rf2+ 4. Kg6 Rg2+ 5. Kf6 Rf2+ 6. Kg5 Rg2+ 7. Rg4 {The rook blocks the check: the bridge is built and the pawn queens.}
key: Check his king away, rook to the fourth rank, king out, then block the check.

### LESSON philidor | Philidor: third-rank defence
goal: Hold rook and pawn against rook with the Philidor defence.
rule: King in front of the pawn, rook on your third rank; when the pawn steps onto it, check from behind.
> This is the most important drawing method in rook endings. Your king stands in front of the pawn, and your rook guards your third rank, so his king cannot come forward. When the pawn steps onto that rank, his king loses its shelter: your rook drops to the back and checks from behind. The checks never end.

#### EX philidor-a | 3k4/8/7r/3PK3/8/8/8/R7 b - - 0 1 | src=book
> Black to move. Your king is in front of the pawn.
1... Rg6! {The rook stays on the sixth rank, your third: White's king cannot reach it. [g:h6-g6]} 2. Ra7 Rb6 3. d6 {The pawn steps onto the sixth rank, so White's king has no shelter in front of it any more.} Rb1! {Now the rook goes to the back and checks from behind. [g:b6-b1]} 4. Ke6 Re1+ 5. Kd5 Rd1+ 6. Kc6 Rc1+ 7. Kd5 {The checks go on forever: a draw.}
key: Rook on your third rank until the pawn arrives there, then check from behind.

### LESSON longside | Check from far away
goal: Hold rook and pawn against rook when Philidor is too late.
rule: King to the short side of the pawn, rook to the long side, and check from far away.
> If his king already stands on your third rank, the Philidor defence is too late. Then your king goes to the short side of the pawn, the side with fewer files to the edge. Your rook goes to the long side and checks from a distance. Checks from close by run out; from far away they never do.

#### EX longside-a | 2k5/R7/4K3/3P4/8/8/8/7r b - - 0 1 | src=book
> Black to move. White's king is already on the sixth rank.
1... Rh6+! {Check from the far side of the board. [g:h1-h6]} 2. Ke5 Rh5+ 3. Ke6 Rh6+ 4. Kf5 {White's king cannot hide from the checks and the pawn cannot advance.}
key: Check from far away; a rook needs distance to keep checking.
